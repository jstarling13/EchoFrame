# EchoFrame Business Blueprint — Revised

## Mission
Help businesses use AI and automation to remove avoidable operating friction while leaving them with systems their own people can understand, operate, and extend.

## Category
Founder-led AI implementation and workflow automation consulting.

## Commercial Model
EchoFrame sells professional implementation time rather than software licenses or fixed public packages.

Current commercial terms:
- $40/hour
- travel quoted per trip
- $50/day onsite food stipend
- no public checkout
- scope defined after a conversation

## Economic Logic
The business creates value when client savings, added capacity, reduced error exposure, improved response speed, or improved visibility materially exceed the cost of implementation.

EchoFrame should quantify value before expanding scope, but it should not promise guaranteed savings.

## Primary Buyer
For the launch lane:
- owner
- managing partner
- finance lead
- office manager
- bookkeeper / CPA acting as trusted introducer
- operations lead

## Core Problems
EchoFrame is most useful when a business has:
- repeated manual entry
- duplicate data handling
- fragmented spreadsheets and inboxes
- delayed reconciliation
- poor multi-entity visibility
- slow lead follow-up
- manual reporting
- inconsistent handoffs
- staff using AI without a governed workflow
- an owner who knows time is being wasted but has not mapped the process

## Service Architecture
### Diagnose
Workflow Discovery & Opportunity Mapping

### Improve
Bookkeeping & Financial Workflow Automation  
Multi-Entity & Intercompany Tracking  
Process & Workflow Automation  
Customer Communication & Lead Follow-Up  
Reporting & Owner Dashboards

### Enable
Staff Training  
Systems & Security Review

### Continue
Ongoing AI Consulting

This architecture is a navigation system, not a set of fixed-price packages.

## Operating Loop
1. Discover
2. Map
3. Measure
4. Prioritize
5. Design
6. Build
7. Test
8. Train
9. Transfer
10. Measure again

## Moat
EchoFrame's defensibility should come from:
- trust with referral partners
- direct delivery
- reusable implementation patterns
- disciplined scoping
- documented workflows
- practical financial/operational fluency
- staying current on AI models and tooling
- accumulated proof from real small-business workflows

It should not rely on proprietary buzzwords or forced vendor lock-in.

## Success Metrics
Internal operating metrics:
- qualified introductions per month
- fit-call to paid-work conversion
- average initial engagement value
- implementation hours by service type
- gross margin after travel/software
- client expansion rate
- referral-partner repeat introductions
- percentage of projects with quantified before/after measures
- support burden per completed implementation
- days from first conversation to first usable result

## Capacity Guardrail
Because delivery is founder-led, EchoFrame must avoid selling support-heavy systems that create indefinite maintenance obligations.

Every build should answer:
- Who owns this after launch?
- What requires EchoFrame?
- What can the client do themselves?
- What breaks if a model/API changes?
- What monitoring is required?
- Is ongoing support economically justified?

## Growth Sequence
1. Prove delivery through warm referrals.
2. Standardize discovery and proposal process.
3. Capture credible case studies.
4. Formalize referral partnerships.
5. Expand selectively into additional verticals.
6. Add repeatable internal tooling only after patterns are clear.
7. Consider subcontractors or additional delivery capacity only when demand and documentation justify it.
