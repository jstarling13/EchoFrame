# EchoFrame Outreach Library

## 1. Warm Introduction Reply
Thanks for the introduction. The easiest place to start is the workflow itself. If you can give me a quick overview of what is taking too much time or requiring too much manual work, I can tell you whether it looks like something worth exploring. I generally start with a short conversation before recommending any build.

## 2. Referral Partner Ask
I'm building EchoFrame around practical AI and workflow automation for small businesses. The best fit is usually a client who has a process everyone already knows is too manual: bookkeeping support, document intake, reconciliation, reporting, customer follow-up, or work spread across email and spreadsheets.

I'm not trying to replace the bookkeeper or CPA. A lot of the value is giving them better systems so they can spend less time on repetitive preparation work.

If you run into a client like that, a simple introduction is enough. I'll handle the scoping directly and tell them if I don't think automation is the right answer.

## 3. Post-Referral Thank You
I appreciate the introduction. I'll keep the conversation focused on the workflow you flagged and I won't pull you into the technical side unless the client wants you involved or your professional input is needed.

## 4. Prospect Follow-Up After Fit Call
Thanks again for walking me through the process. Based on the conversation, the first useful step is [paid discovery / scoped implementation]. I'll send a short written scope covering the objective, what I need access to, the expected hours or range, and the commercial terms. I won't expand the work beyond that without discussing it first.

## 5. No-Fit Follow-Up
Thanks for the conversation. Based on what you described, I don't think a custom EchoFrame project is the best first move. The problem appears better suited to [existing software / process cleanup / professional review]. If that changes later and the bottleneck becomes a workflow/integration issue, I'm happy to revisit it.
