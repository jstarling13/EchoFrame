# EchoFrame Objection Playbook

## "We can do this ourselves."
Response:
"That may be the right answer. The question is whether the team has the time to map the workflow, test it against real exceptions, connect the systems, document it, and maintain it. If they do, I don't need to manufacture a consulting project."

## "AI makes mistakes."
Response:
"Correct, which is why I don't treat AI as an authority. The workflow should be designed around what the model is allowed to do, what must be validated, and where a person approves the result."

## "Our data is sensitive."
Response:
"Then the first question is the data path, not the model. We need to know what information is involved, who can access it, what vendors receive it, and whether the workflow can be designed with acceptable controls."

## "We don't have time for a project."
Response:
"Then the first scope has to be small. I would rather fix one painful workflow with minimal disruption than create a large transformation program your staff cannot absorb."

## "Why are you only $40 an hour?"
Response:
"The rate is intentionally simple. You're paying for the work performed, with travel handled separately. The more important question is whether the project creates enough value to justify the hours."

Do not apologize for the rate or imply low quality.

## "Why not just buy software?"
Response:
"If a proven product already solves the problem cleanly, you probably should. My role is useful when the problem sits between systems, requires workflow redesign, or needs implementation around how your business actually works."

## "Which AI model is best?"
Response:
"There isn't one answer independent of the task. I choose tools based on the workflow, data, reliability requirement, cost, and how the system needs to be maintained."

## "Will this replace employees?"
Response:
"The goal is to remove repetitive work and improve capacity. Staffing decisions belong to the business. EchoFrame is not sold as a headcount-replacement service."

## "Can you guarantee savings?"
Response:
"No. I can estimate value using the current workflow and conservative assumptions, then measure results after implementation."

## "Can you automate the whole thing?"
Response:
"Usually the better design automates the routine path and gives a person a clean exception queue. Trying to automate every edge case is often more expensive and less reliable."
