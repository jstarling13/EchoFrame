After-Hours Capture Note

Of the 21 calls that landed outside business hours this week, every one was answered live — none dropped to voicemail. Call Router qualifies each by urgency: true emergencies (buzzing breakers, partial outages, burning smells) trigger an immediate SMS to the on-call tech with the caller's name, address, and symptom, while non-urgent estimate requests are captured and added to the next-morning call-back list so techs aren't woken for a deck-lighting quote.

Industry data is blunt on this: roughly 1 in 4 service calls comes in after hours, and the average homeowner with an electrical problem calls the next contractor within minutes if no one picks up. The 21 captured calls represent demand that, on a voicemail-only line, would have largely been lost to a competitor before morning.

Sources: Bridgeway Electric call records (June 1–7, 2026) · EchoFrame Call Router qualification log · home-services after-hours call benchmarks (Q2 2026). Job values are conservative Columbus, GA averages.

The One Thing

The after-hours calls alone were worth about $14,200 in booked work this week.

The 21 captured after-hours calls converted into 11 booked jobs — a mix of two emergency panel/breaker repairs (~$1,800 each), service calls (~$350 each), and two larger estimate-stage jobs (an EV charger and a panel upgrade) that started with an after-hours call. At a conservative blended value, that's roughly $14,200 in work that, on a voicemail-only line, would most likely have gone to the next contractor before you opened. Over a year, that single capture rate is the difference between a busy schedule and a leaking one — worth on the order of $70K+ in recovered annual revenue.
