Pattern Note

The blended 32.6% labor rate looks healthy — but it hides a clear daypart pattern: mornings carry the business and late afternoons bleed it. Every morning shift runs near 22% labor and throws off the bulk of weekly margin. After 2 PM, foot traffic falls off a cliff while staffing stays flat at two baristas, so labor climbs to 47% by afternoon and past 100% in the weeknight evening — the only shift that loses money outright. Weekends repeat the shape on a smaller scale. The issue isn't wage rates; it's hours scheduled against demand in the back half of the day.

Method: each shift's scheduled labor (wages + ~18% payroll burden) measured against POS revenue for the same hours. Targets from NRA / IBISWorld limited-service coffee benchmarks (28–32% labor). EchoFrame shift index, Q2 2026.

The One Thing

Close at 5 PM on weekdays and run the 2–5 PM block with one barista.

The weeknight evening shift is the only one on the board that loses money — about $465/week in labor against just $360 in sales. Cutting it and dropping the slow weekday afternoon from two baristas to one removes roughly $695/week of labor — about $3,000 a month. You give up only the ~$360/week of lowest-margin evening revenue, much of which shifts to earlier orders, for an estimated net savings of ~$2,300 a month with no change to your busy, profitable mornings.
