# Pitch Scripts — EchoFrame

Three versions for three contexts. Read each one out loud before using it. If it doesn't sound like you talking, change a word until it does.

The voice across all three: direct, confident, no jargon, no apology. You're not selling them software. You're handing them an answer to a question they've been trying to answer on their own.

---

## 1. Elevator Pitch — 30 Seconds

**Use this when:** Networking event. Someone asks "what do you do?" Coffee. Hangar talk.

> "I help small business owners actually understand their numbers. The way it usually works is they get a P&L from their accountant, they nod, and they go back to running on gut. I put together a one page report every month that tells them in plain English where the money is going, what's leaking, and the one thing they should fix this month. Mostly home services and restaurants right now. The whole point is to have the conversation an owner wishes their accountant would have, but doesn't."

**Why it works:** Names a real pain in 8 seconds (P&L they nod at). Names the deliverable in 8 more (one page report, plain English). Names the outcome in 8 more (the conversation their accountant doesn't have). Leaves room for follow up: "what kind of businesses?" or "how much does that cost?"

**If they ask what it costs:** "$150 a month for the recurring report. One time projects are $299 to $899 depending on what you need."

---

## 2. Cold Call Opener — 15 Seconds

**Use this when:** They picked up the phone. You have about 8 seconds before they decide whether to hang up.

> "Hey [First Name], this is [Your Name]. Quick reason for the call — I work with [HVAC / restaurant / salon] owners on the financial side. Most of them tell me they don't really know which of their jobs make money. I put together a free sample report on businesses like yours and wanted to send you one. Got 30 seconds for me to explain how it works?"

**Why it works:**
- Names yourself first so they don't think it's a robocall
- "Quick reason for the call" tells them you're not going to waste their time
- Names a specific pain ("don't really know which of their jobs make money") that's true for almost every owner
- "Free sample report" is the offer, stated up front
- "30 seconds" is a small ask that's easy to say yes to

**If they say "I'm busy":** "Totally fair. I'll text you the one page version. What's the best number?"

**If they say "what's it cost":** "Sample report is free. If you want recurring after that, it's $150 a month. But the sample's free either way."

**If they say "send me an email":** "Will do. What's the best email? I'll send it the same day."

---

## 3. Sales Call Opener — 60 Seconds

**Use this when:** They booked a 15 or 30 minute call with you. First minute sets the whole tone.

> "Hey [First Name], thanks for making time. Before I jump in, here's what I want to do with the next 15 minutes so we don't waste each other's time.
>
> First, I want to ask you four or five questions about [Company Name] — what you sell, how you're staffed, what's working, what's frustrating you on the financial side. That'll take about five minutes.
>
> Then I want to walk you through a sample report I built for a business like yours. So you can see the actual format, what's in it, and what you'd get every month. That's another five.
>
> Then we'll talk about whether it's worth doing for [Company Name] specifically, and if it is, how that works. Five minutes on that.
>
> The reason I run calls this way is the report is the whole product. Once you've seen one, you either want one for your business or you don't. I'd rather you see it first and decide based on what's actually useful, not on a pitch.
>
> Sound good? Let's start with what you actually do day to day at [Company Name]."

**Why it works:**
- "So we don't waste each other's time" signals respect
- Naming the structure up front removes the "is this a 30 minute pitch?" anxiety
- "The report is the whole product" frames the entire conversation around the deliverable, not the price
- "Decide based on what's actually useful" reframes you as someone who's not desperate for the sale
- Ending with a question ("what you actually do day to day") gets them talking, which is what you want

**If they push back on the structure ("just send me pricing"):** "Happy to. The recurring report is $150 a month, audits are $499, competitor reports are $299. But honestly, none of that matters until you see what one looks like. Want to spend 60 seconds looking at a sample so you know what we're talking about?"

## Tone Rules — Apply to All Three

- **Don't apologize.** Not for taking their time, not for the cold reach, not for the price. You're solving a problem they have. Act like it.
- **Don't oversell.** Don't say "transformative," "game changing," or "robust." Say what it is and let them decide.
- **Pause after the offer.** When you say "I'd send you a free sample," stop talking. Let them respond.
- **If they go silent for 3+ seconds on the phone, count to five before you fill the gap.** Most people fill silence with a yes.
- **Mirror their language.** If they call it a "P&L," call it a P&L. If they call it "the books," call it the books. Match them, don't correct them.
- **End every call with a clear next step and a date.** "I'll send the sample by end of day Wednesday and follow up Friday morning" beats "I'll get it over to you soon."

## What to Never Say

- "We leverage..."
- "Our solution..."
- "Game changing"
- "Synergies"
- "Best in class"
- "Cutting edge"
- "Robust"
- "I just wanted to..."
- "Sorry to bother you"
- "If you have a minute..."

If any of those slip out, stop, restart, and use plainer words.
