# EchoFrame — Marketing Plan

## The one thing to fix first: positioning

Lead with ONE promise everywhere. Demote the rest to supporting proof.

**Hero promise:**
> EchoFrame reads your business's money and tells you, in plain English, what's going on — and the #1 thing to fix to keep more of it.

**Supporting features (mention as proof, never as the headline):**
- Auto-organizes every dollar so nothing's a guess
- Flags when you're overpaying a vendor or bill
- A 5-minute, plain-English report — no spreadsheets, no jargon

**Set aside for now** (they muddy the message): competitor price-watch, scheduling/which-days-make-money. Bring them back later as upsells once people know what EchoFrame *is*.

---

## Track 1 — Referral ads (recruit "embedded" referrers)

**Who to target — not "everyone under 25."** Target young people who are *surrounded by* business owners:
- **Employees** of small businesses (servers, shop clerks, salon assistants, dispatchers) — they see the owner stressed and the owner trusts them. Best referrer.
- **Family** — kids, nieces, nephews of owners. Warmest possible intro for a money tool.
- **Business/accounting students** & biz-adjacent young workers (real estate assistants, people running socials for local shops).

**Three rules that matter more than wording:**
1. Lead with the payout + a *specific* relationship, not generic "easy money."
2. Give each referrer a **unique link/code** — never "DM me a name."
3. Pay **per paying customer**, not per signup (so you don't pay for tire-kickers). Frame it as a clean affiliate bounty, not "recruit people."

### Ad A — Employee
> **Work for a small business? Your boss is probably stressed about money. Easy $25.**
> EchoFrame reads their numbers and tells them in plain English what's going on and the one thing to fix. Takes 5 minutes to read.
> Send your link → they become a customer → you get $25.
> [your link]

### Ad B — Family
> **Your parents own a business? This is the easiest $25 you'll make.**
> EchoFrame shows them — in plain English — where every dollar's going and the #1 thing to fix to keep more of it. No spreadsheets, no jargon.
> Send your link → they sign up → $25 lands.
> [your link]

### Ad C — Business / accounting student
> **Know small business owners? Turn that into $25 a pop.**
> EchoFrame is the plain-English money read every owner wishes they had — what's happening with their cash and exactly what to fix.
> Drop your link → they become a customer → you get paid. Every time.
> [your link]

**Make sharing effortless:** give referrers an *asset* to send, not a pitch to recite — a 20-second demo clip or a one-screen sample report. People forward assets; they don't recite sales pitches.

## Track 2 — Direct-to-owner campaign (run in parallel)

Often cheaper per customer than paying a $25 bounty + ad spend to reach a referrer to reach an owner. Run both, see which wins.

**Targeting (Meta):**
- Interest/behavior: "Small business owners," Facebook Page admins, "Business owners"
- Job titles: owner, founder, proprietor
- Layer by local geography + relevant industries (food, retail, salons, trades)

**Hook angles to A/B (all lead with the hero promise):**
- *Pain:* "Stressed about money but can't say exactly why? EchoFrame tells you — in plain English — the one thing to change."
- *Time/ease:* "Your whole business's money, explained in a 5-minute read. No spreadsheets."
- *Specific win:* "It caught a vendor overcharging me. Paid for itself the first week." (only if true / testimonial)

**Structure of a direct-to-owner ad:**
> **Stressed about your business's money but can't say exactly why?**
> EchoFrame reads your numbers and tells you, in plain English, what's going on and the #1 thing to fix to keep more of it. Five-minute read — no spreadsheets, no jargon.
> See it → echoframe.net

**Mechanic:** straight to echoframe.net (or a landing page that mirrors the ad's exact promise — message match is critical or you lose the click you paid for).

---

## How to test (don't guess — let the numbers talk)
- Run **Track 1 vs Track 2** side by side with small budgets first.
- Within each, test **2–3 hooks**; kill losers fast, scale the winner.
- Track **cost per paying customer**, not clicks or signups.
- Watch for the referral funnel's failure mode: people who want the $25 but know no owners. If referral cost-per-customer is way above direct, shift budget to direct.

## Honest cautions
- "Refer + get paid + recruit others" can read as MLM and trip Meta ad review. The per-paying-customer affiliate framing keeps it clean.
- Message match: every ad's promise must match the landing page's first screen, or you pay for clicks that bounce.
- Only the features you can actually deliver well should be in the copy.
