Our Story

Columbus runs on its small businesses.

The restaurants, the salons, the shops, the contractors. The places we grew up around, and the people who keep this community working.

You're really good at what you do. You've put in the years, you know your craft cold, and your customers can feel it the second you start.

So why does the money side of your business feel like a language nobody ever taught you?

Here's the honest answer. Being great at your work and being great at your books are two totally different skills. Almost nobody is great at both, and nobody ever sat you down and taught you the second one.

I saw this up close with my wife. She does hair and makeup and she's incredible at it. People get out of her chair looking like the best version of themselves. That talent took years, and you can't fake it. But the business behind it is a whole different thing. She can quote a client a price in two seconds flat. Ask her whether that appointment actually made money after product and time, and you'll get an honest shrug. That part was never hers, and it was never going to be. She wants to do hair. She doesn't want to sit at the kitchen table at 10pm trying to make a spreadsheet tell her whether the month went okay.

And here's the thing. She's not the exception. She's the rule. All over Columbus there are people who are the best in town at what they do, cutting and cooking and building and fixing, who never got handed the other half of the job. The half where you're suddenly supposed to read your own business like an accountant. Nobody should have to be great at both.

Here's why that matters to you. When you can't see where your money is actually going, you end up making the big calls on gut feel. And gut feel is expensive. A leak you can't see is one you keep paying for, month after month.

That's why I built EchoFrame. Your accountant keeps your books clean and your taxes filed right, and that work matters. But it answers a different question than the one you're actually asking, which is this: what part of my business is quietly losing money right now, and what do I do about it this month? Once a month, EchoFrame takes your own numbers and hands them back as a short, plain English read on what's really going on, and it ends with the one thing worth your time. It's not some giant report you'll never open. Every number gets compared to other businesses around Columbus, not a national average that has no idea what things cost here. It sits right next to the work your accountant already does. It doesn't replace it.

Think of it as the base layer. The owners who need this clarity the most are the newest and smallest, the ones who were never big enough for a CFO or a full time accountant. EchoFrame is built for exactly that stage. And when you grow into a CPA, you'll walk in already knowing your numbers instead of handing over a shoebox.

And this goes way past any one shop. When local businesses run a little tighter and a little clearer, they stay open. They keep people on payroll. They keep money moving right here in Columbus instead of somewhere else.

A stronger set of local businesses is a stronger Columbus.
