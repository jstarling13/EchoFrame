Our Story

Columbus runs on its small businesses.

The restaurants, the salons, the shops, the contractors — the places we grew up around, and the people who keep this community working.

Running a small business is hard in a way that's easy to miss from the outside. A lot of owners here are working themselves to the bone, doing everything right, and still feeling like they can't quite tell where their money is going.

Their accountant does important work — keeping the books clean and the filings correct. But that work answers a different question than the one owners are asking day to day: which part of the business is quietly losing money right now, and what should I do about it this month? Without that, big decisions end up getting made on gut feel, because gut feel is what's in front of them.

That gap is what EchoFrame exists to close. Once a month, a business's own numbers come back as a short, clear breakdown of what's actually happening — ending with one specific thing worth looking at. Written in normal language, not a thirty-page deck. Every comparison is to other Columbus-area businesses rather than a national average that doesn't reflect what things actually cost here. It's meant to sit alongside the work an accountant already does, not replace it.

The reason this matters goes beyond any single shop. When these businesses are run a little tighter and a little clearer, the whole community benefits. They stay open. They keep people employed. They keep money moving here in Columbus rather than somewhere else.

A stronger set of local businesses is a stronger Columbus.
