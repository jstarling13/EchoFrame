Why EchoFrame exists

Small business runs on people like you.

The restaurants, the salons, the shops, the contractors. The people who are great at the work, and were never taught the other half of the job.

The gap

Great at the work. Never taught the books.

You are really good at what you do. You have put in the years, you know your craft cold, and your customers can feel it the second you start. So why does the money side of the business feel like a language nobody ever taught you?

Here is the honest answer. Being great at your work and being great at your books are two totally different skills. Almost nobody is great at both, and nobody ever sat you down and taught you the second one.

Founder Jacob Starling saw this up close with his wife. She does hair and makeup and is incredible at it. People get out of her chair looking like the best version of themselves. That talent took years. But the business behind it is a whole different thing: she can quote a client a price in two seconds flat. Ask her whether that appointment actually made money after product and time, and you will get an honest shrug. She wants to do hair. She does not want to sit at the kitchen table at 10pm trying to make a spreadsheet tell her whether the month went okay.

She is not the exception. All over the country there are people who are the best at what they do, cutting and cooking and building and fixing, who never got handed the other half of the job. Nobody should have to be great at both.

Jacob Starling, Founder

The base layer, before you can afford a CFO.

When you cannot see where your money is actually going, you end up making the big calls on gut feel, and gut feel is expensive. A leak you cannot see is one you keep paying for, month after month. That is why Jacob built EchoFrame.

Your accountant keeps your books clean and your taxes filed right, and that work matters. But it answers a different question than the one you are actually asking: what part of my business is quietly losing money right now, and what do I do about it this month?

Once a month, EchoFrame takes your own numbers and hands them back as a short, plain English read on what is really going on, ending with the one thing worth your time. Every number is compared to real businesses in your industry, not a generic national average. Think of it as the base layer: the owners who need this clarity most are the newest and smallest, the ones who were never big enough for a CFO or a full time accountant. When you grow into a CPA, you walk in already knowing your numbers instead of handing over a shoebox.
