# EchoFrame Information Architecture — Clean, Consulting-Style

## Design Principle
The site should feel like a consulting firm that has made difficult information easy to navigate.

Use:
- bold section headlines
- fewer words visible at once
- progressive disclosure
- clickable categories
- clear grouping
- strong whitespace
- short supporting copy
- visual hierarchy before decoration

Avoid:
- giant wall of service cards
- dozens of equally weighted links
- dashboard-like density
- SaaS-feature-grid aesthetics
- overly animated UI
- copied Bain colors or text

# Recommended Top-Level Navigation
- Services
- Industries
- Method
- About
- Contact

Secondary/footer:
- Training
- Security
- Privacy
- Terms

If Training/Security are strategically important, they can remain top-level, but Claude should test whether navigation is becoming crowded.

# Homepage Logic
The homepage should answer in sequence:
1. What does EchoFrame do?
2. What kind of business problem does it solve?
3. Does it understand my industry/context?
4. What can it help with?
5. How does it work?
6. Why trust Jacob/EchoFrame?
7. What does it cost / how do I start?
8. CTA

# Industry Experience
Do not make the user read every industry. Let them choose.

Question 1:
**What kind of business are you running?**

Question 2:
**What is taking too much time?**

Then show:
**A practical place to start**

This keeps the site interactive without pretending to perform a full diagnostic.
