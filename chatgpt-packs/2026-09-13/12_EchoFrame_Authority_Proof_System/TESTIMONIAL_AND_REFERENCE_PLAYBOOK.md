# Testimonial and Reference Playbook

## Decision for this client

Do not request or publish a public testimonial. The client is Jacob’s aunt. Jacob chose to use her only as a private reference for qualified prospects. Never describe her as an unrelated client.

## Low-friction reference request

**Text or email subject:** Quick permission question

Hi [CLIENT FIRST NAME],

I’m putting together a more complete explanation of the accounting automation work I built for you. I’m keeping the business anonymous and won’t publish your name, the company name, the franchise brands, or private financial details.

Would you be comfortable serving as a private reference for a serious prospective client if I ask you first each time? I would never share your contact information without checking with you, and you could always decline.

I’d also like to confirm the operating figures before I use them anywhere: approximately 48 hours per week before, approximately six hours per week after, observed from June through September 2026. Please correct anything that is not accurate.

Thank you,
Jacob

## Prospect-facing copy

**Private reference available after qualification.** Because this engagement involved a family member and confidential operating information, EchoFrame does not use the client’s praise as a public testimonial. For a serious prospective client evaluating comparable work, a private reference may be available with the client’s specific permission.

## Introduction email

Subject: Reference introduction: [PROSPECT] and [CLIENT]

Hi [CLIENT] and [PROSPECT],

Thank you both for agreeing to connect. [CLIENT] worked with me on an accounting-automation project for a multi-location operation. For transparency, [CLIENT] is also my aunt. She can speak directly to what the workflow looked like before, what I built, how it performed in use, and what required adjustment.

I’ll leave the two of you to choose a convenient time. Please keep any business details shared on the call confidential.

Jacob

## Guardrails

- Get permission before every introduction.
- Disclose the family relationship before the prospect accepts the reference.
- Do not coach the client on what to say.
- Do not publish a quote later without separate written permission for the exact wording and disclosure.
- Do not offer the reference until the prospect is credible, the need is comparable, and a reference call would materially help the decision.
