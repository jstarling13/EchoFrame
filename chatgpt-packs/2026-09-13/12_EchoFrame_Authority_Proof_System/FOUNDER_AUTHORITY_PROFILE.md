# Judgment Before Automation

Jacob Starling founded EchoFrame around a simple conviction: a system is only useful if its assumptions survive contact with the real work.

That conviction came from more than experimenting with new software. In his first automation engagement, Jacob watched one accounting operator spend three 16-hour days each week completing a multi-location financial workflow by hand. He did not begin by selling a platform. He mapped the repeated posting work, built against QuickBooks Desktop’s integration path, and kept refining the system after the first version exposed its limits. The result was a client-tracked reduction from approximately 48 hours to six hours per week over a roughly three-month period. `[CONFIRM WITH JACOB]` The result was not independently audited, and EchoFrame does not present it as a universal outcome.

The same habit of challenging the first answer shaped Jacob’s finance work at Emory University’s Goizueta Business School. In one valuation exercise, an initial Comcast model produced a value of $57.11 per share. Rather than treating a precise output as a correct output, Jacob reconsidered the peer group, replaced media-heavy comparables with businesses that better reflected Comcast’s connectivity economics, normalized beta from 0.655 to 1.00, and revised margin and capital-expenditure assumptions. The resulting range, $29.75 to $35.74, was less dramatic and more defensible. `[CONFIRM WITH JACOB: academic context, dates, and exact figures]`

That example captures how Jacob approaches automation: outputs inherit the weaknesses of their inputs. A polished model, dashboard, or AI response is not evidence by itself. The underlying data, assumptions, controls, and operating context have to make sense.

Jacob also served as an analyst with Blue Eagle Capital, where he developed institutional-style investment memos and pitch decks, presented recommendations to an investment committee, and monitored approved positions against thesis-specific KPIs and catalysts. `[CONFIRM WITH JACOB: exact role wording and whether “secured approvals” may be stated publicly]` That experience reinforced a second principle behind EchoFrame: the work does not end when a recommendation is presented. Someone must remain accountable for whether it performs as expected and for recognizing when the facts change.

Jacob holds an M.S. in Finance from Emory University. He has also completed independent-contractor work that includes building a live professional-services website and developing additional, undeployed website builds and operating documentation. Because those clients and projects are private, EchoFrame does not present them as named case studies or attach performance claims that were never measured.

EchoFrame brings those disciplines together: observe the actual process, test the assumptions, build the smallest reliable intervention, measure what changed, and leave the client able to operate it. Jacob stays directly involved from discovery through implementation and transfer. There is no handoff to a sales team or rotating delivery staff.

The point is not to make a small business look more technologically sophisticated. It is to help the business operate with less friction, clearer control, and less dependence on repetitive work or outside vendors.

## Short founder bio

Jacob Starling is the founder of EchoFrame, a hands-on AI and workflow-automation consultancy based in the Columbus, Georgia area. He holds an M.S. in Finance from Emory University and applies the same discipline used in investment analysis to operating systems: challenge the inputs, make risk visible, measure the result, and remain accountable after implementation.
