# EchoFrame Master Brief

## Business
EchoFrame is a founder-led AI implementation and workflow automation consulting practice operated by Jacob Starling.

It is a separate business from Magnolia Grove Consultants and must remain separate in strategy, branding, files, clients, and marketing.

The active EchoFrame consulting practice is also separate from Jacob's prior EchoFrame product venture. Only the EchoFrame name and logo are being reused. Old product names and product-market positioning are not part of this practice.

## Current Stage
Pre-launch. A working Next.js site exists locally and has already been rebranded to EchoFrame. Deployment, final domain connection, legal structure, and several commercial operating documents remain incomplete.

## Core Commercial Model
- $40/hour for actual work performed.
- Travel fee quoted per trip before booking.
- $50/day onsite food stipend.
- No fixed public packages.
- No public checkout.
- Each engagement is scoped after a conversation.
- Ongoing support can continue month-to-month at the same hourly model unless Jacob later approves another structure.

## What EchoFrame Does
EchoFrame helps small and lower-mid-market businesses remove repetitive administrative work, connect fragmented workflows, make better use of existing software, and apply AI where it creates practical operating value.

The practice is implementation-oriented. Strategy is used to decide what should be built, but the business is not positioned as a strategy-only advisory firm.

Primary capabilities include:
- workflow discovery and opportunity mapping
- bookkeeping and financial workflow automation
- multi-entity tracking and intercompany workflow support
- process and workflow automation
- customer communication and lead follow-up
- reporting and owner dashboards
- staff AI/tool training
- systems and access review
- ongoing AI consulting and optimization

## Positioning
EchoFrame should be understood as:
**Practical AI implementation and workflow automation for businesses that need working systems, not AI theater.**

The brand should emphasize:
- hands-on delivery
- direct founder involvement
- practical systems built around how the business actually operates
- client ownership and transferability
- transparent hourly billing
- current knowledge of fast-moving AI tools
- measured use of AI, not forcing AI into every process

## Primary Launch ICP — Recommended
Owner-led or closely managed businesses, generally 2–50 employees, where:
- the buyer is easy to identify
- manual administrative work is visible
- spreadsheets, email, accounting systems, and disconnected tools create repetitive effort
- there is at least one workflow with measurable time cost or error risk
- the owner, bookkeeper, CPA, office manager, operations leader, or finance lead can sponsor change
- the organization can provide process access and a real subject-matter owner
- implementation can be scoped without enterprise procurement overhead

The strongest immediate channel is referral through trusted bookkeepers, CPAs, and other professional advisors, especially in the Northeast / New York City market.

## Expansion ICP — Recommended
Established businesses with roughly 51–250 employees remain attractive when:
- an executive sponsor exists
- a process owner is available
- there is a defined workflow backlog
- the company can support implementation governance
- the engagement can remain founder-led without creating an unrealistic support burden

This lane should not drive launch messaging until the primary referral channel is working consistently.

## Initial High-Fit Industries
1. Accounting and bookkeeping-adjacent businesses
2. Professional practices
3. Multi-entity owner/operators
4. Commercial real estate and property administration
5. Specialty contractors and construction administration
6. Recruiting / staffing and other document-heavy B2B services
7. Established local and regional service businesses with repetitive back-office work

## What EchoFrame Is Not
- Not a replacement for bookkeepers or CPAs.
- Not a managed IT provider.
- Not an enterprise systems integrator.
- Not a generic "AI agency" selling prompts.
- Not a SaaS product catalog.
- Not a vendor that forces clients into proprietary lock-in.
- Not a provider of autonomous high-risk decisions in healthcare, lending, employment, legal, investment, or other regulated contexts.

## Founder Positioning
Jacob is the delivery lead and should be presented honestly as a hands-on consultant who stays current on rapidly changing AI capabilities and builds systems directly with clients.

The business should not pretend to have a large staff or institutional scale it does not have.

The strongest framing is:
**Direct expertise, direct accountability, and implementation without layers of account management.**

## Delivery Principle
Every engagement should follow:
1. Understand the workflow as it actually operates.
2. Quantify the cost, friction, risk, or delay.
3. Identify what should and should not be automated.
4. Build the smallest useful solution.
5. Test with real users and real exceptions.
6. Train the people who will own it.
7. Document the system.
8. Measure results and improve only where justified.

## Primary Growth Motion
The launch motion should be:
trusted referral partner → owner introduction → short fit conversation → paid discovery / scoped initial work when appropriate → implementation → proof of value → expansion or ongoing support.

The business should avoid building a large marketing engine before the referral motion and delivery process are working reliably.
