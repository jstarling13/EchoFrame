# EchoFrame ICP, Personas & Buying Triggers

# Lane A — Primary Launch ICP

## Company Profile
Generally:
- 2–50 employees
- owner-led or closely managed
- established enough to have recurring operating processes
- administrative work matters to margin/capacity
- systems are usable but poorly connected
- decision-maker is accessible
- process owner exists
- project can start without enterprise procurement

## Common Operating Pattern
- QuickBooks/accounting system + spreadsheets
- email as a workflow engine
- manual document intake
- repeated copying between tools
- recurring owner reports assembled by hand
- inconsistent customer follow-up
- one employee who knows how everything works

## Strong Fit Statements
- We do this manually every day.
- My bookkeeper is buried.
- Only one person knows this process.
- We keep copying the same information.
- We need another admin person.
- We have multiple entities and no clean view.
- Our leads sit too long.
- We tried AI, but it never became a real workflow.

# Lane B — Expansion ICP
Generally 51–250 employees when:
- executive sponsor exists
- process owner exists
- workflow is bounded
- security/procurement is manageable
- project does not require a delivery team EchoFrame does not have
- support burden is realistic

# Personas

## Owner / Managing Partner
Wants more capacity, fewer bottlenecks, better visibility, clearer accountability, and faster execution.

Fears disruption, overspending, AI hype, data risk, and vendor lock-in.

## Bookkeeper / CPA / Finance Professional
Role: trusted introducer, process expert, reviewer.

Wants less repetitive prep/matching, cleaner inputs, exception-focused work, more capacity, and preserved professional control.

Positioning rule: never imply this persona is obsolete.

## Office / Operations Manager
Wants fewer handoffs, less chasing, repeatability, documentation, and fewer workarounds.

## Finance / Accounting Lead
Wants controls, traceability, better reconciliation workflow, cleaner reporting, and clear human approval.

# Buying Triggers
- key employee overloaded/leaving
- entity/location growth
- backlog
- margin pressure
- owner recognizes labor inefficiency
- new system rollout
- recurring service failures
- slow lead response
- reporting delay
- trusted advisor identifies the problem
- merger/acquisition/integration
- software renewal

# Qualification Factors
1. measurable pain
2. sponsor
3. process owner
4. recurrence
5. economics
6. system/data access
7. process stability
8. manageable risk
9. client cooperation
10. transferable support model

# Disqualifiers
- no sponsor
- no process owner
- magic AI expectation
- no access
- no realistic budget
- guaranteed-return demand
- unsupported high-risk use
- desire to bypass professional controls
- indefinite support expectation
- custom build where existing software is obviously better

# Geography
Initial commercial emphasis: New York / Northeast warm referral network.

Delivery: remote-first, with paid onsite travel where useful.

Geography is a go-to-market focus, not a hard service boundary.
