# EchoFrame Entity Options & Questions for an Attorney / CPA

## Purpose
This document is not a recommendation to choose a particular legal structure. It is a plain-language briefing so Jacob can have a more productive conversation with a licensed attorney and CPA.

# Option 1 — Sole Proprietorship

## Plain-language concept
The business is operated directly by the individual rather than through a separate LLC or corporation.

## Potential advantages to ask about
- simplest setup
- low formation/admin burden
- no separate entity filing in some situations
- business income typically flows directly to the owner for tax reporting

## Potential disadvantages to ask about
- no LLC liability shield
- contracts are entered into personally
- business and personal legal exposure may be less separated
- can look less formal to some commercial clients
- may complicate bringing in an owner later

## Questions for counsel / CPA
- Given the type of implementation work EchoFrame performs, is operating without an LLC prudent?
- What personal liability exposures remain even with an LLC?
- What insurance would still be necessary either way?
- What registrations or assumed-name filings would be required where the business operates?
- How should business banking and bookkeeping be handled?

# Option 2 — Single-Member LLC

## Plain-language concept
A separate legal entity with one owner. Tax treatment and legal entity status are separate questions.

## Potential advantages to ask about
- clearer separation between owner and business
- commonly used for small consulting practices
- can make contracting/banking/vendor relationships cleaner
- flexible federal tax classification depending on elections and facts
- easier path to adding formal business processes

## Potential disadvantages to ask about
- formation and annual compliance costs
- state-specific filings and fees
- maintaining separation matters
- an LLC does not eliminate personal liability for every act
- tax treatment may not be meaningfully different unless a different election is appropriate

## Questions for counsel / CPA
- In which state should the LLC actually be formed?
- Does forming outside the owner's home state create unnecessary foreign-registration obligations?
- Where will EchoFrame be considered to be doing business?
- Are New York registrations triggered merely by having NY clients, or only by particular activities/presence?
- Should the default federal tax treatment be retained initially?
- At what income level, if any, should an S-corporation election be evaluated?
- What payroll/admin burden would an S election create?
- Which expenses are properly business expenses?
- How should travel reimbursements and client-paid travel be handled?

# Option 3 — LLC With S-Corporation Tax Election

## Plain-language concept
The legal entity remains an LLC, but it elects a different federal tax treatment if eligible.

## Potential advantages to discuss
- may be tax-efficient at sufficient profit levels in some circumstances

## Potential disadvantages to discuss
- payroll obligations
- reasonable-compensation issues
- added bookkeeping/tax preparation cost
- state-specific consequences
- often not worth the complexity at low or inconsistent profit levels

## Questions for CPA
- At EchoFrame's expected profit level, is this worth modeling?
- What would reasonable compensation likely require?
- What payroll, unemployment, state, and filing obligations would arise?
- What annual profit threshold would make the added complexity economically worthwhile?

# Option 4 — Corporation

## Plain-language concept
A corporation is a separate legal structure with more formal governance.

## Why to discuss rather than assume
A corporation can make sense for businesses expecting outside investment, multiple owners, equity compensation, or certain tax/legal structures. That may be unnecessary for a founder-led consulting practice.

## Questions
- Is there any concrete reason EchoFrame needs a corporation now?
- Would future investment, ownership changes, or product development change that answer?

# Cross-Cutting Questions

## Formation
- What exact entity name should be registered?
- Has the EchoFrame name been cleared sufficiently for entity/trademark purposes?
- Are DBAs/assumed names needed?
- What registered-agent arrangement is appropriate?

## State / Nexus
- Where is the business legally located?
- Where is work physically performed?
- Where are clients located?
- What activities trigger foreign registration in another state?
- What activities create state/local tax filing obligations?

## Contracts
- What legal entity should appear on contracts?
- When should the owner sign personally versus as an authorized representative?
- Are limitation-of-liability and indemnity clauses appropriate for this service model?

## Tax
- How are consulting services treated for state/local sales tax in each jurisdiction where clients are located?
- Does software/API pass-through change treatment?
- Does custom automation ever get characterized differently from consulting?
- What records must be retained to support the treatment?

## Insurance
- General liability?
- Professional liability / E&O?
- Cyber liability?
- Business property/equipment?
- Commercial auto or hired/non-owned auto for client travel?
- Any client-specific certificate-of-insurance requirements?

## Public-Service / Outside-Employment Considerations
If Jacob becomes subject to employer, government, military, or ethics restrictions on outside employment, ask the appropriate ethics/legal office what approvals or limitations apply before continuing client work.

# Decision Standard
Choose the structure based on actual legal risk, actual tax economics, state compliance, administrative burden, and future business plans. Do not choose solely because one entity type sounds more professional.
